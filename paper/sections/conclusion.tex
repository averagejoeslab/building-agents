\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

An agent is a model wrapped in a harness, and the harness has five parts: \prim{Input}, \prim{Context}, \prim{Model interface}, \prim{Output} and \prim{Control flow}. Each is the smallest program that adds it, and each removes a failure the others do not. What distinguishes one harness from another is the design decisions made within those primitives, and what makes a harness fit to run is the answers it gives to six questions asked of each. Nineteen harnesses in wide use, six read from source at pinned versions and thirteen from their sources or documentation at summary level, fit this description with nothing left over. The vocabulary lets a result be reported with the harness that produced it, lets research compare primitives by their implementations, and lets an engineer isolate a failure to the primitives an implementation touches. The comparison of implementations is the work that remains, and it is also the test of the framework.
